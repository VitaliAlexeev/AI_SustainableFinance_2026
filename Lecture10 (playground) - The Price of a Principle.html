The optimiser's price curve

Each position of the slider is a real optimisation: the lowest tracking error that cuts portfolio ESG risk to the target. Watch the marginal price climb, and the Sharpe ratio stay flat — until it does not.

Predict first. Up to what cut in ESG risk does the Sharpe ratio stay within 1% of the benchmark's?
About a third: the Sharpe ratio is still at 0.99 of the benchmark's at a 33% cut. At a 59% cut it is 0.83. Under benchmark-implied returns the relative Sharpe ratio equals the correlation with the benchmark, so the price of the first improvements is almost nothing.

Optimise for one rater, judged by the other

Unrelated constraints add like independent risks: √(0.81² + 0.41²) = 0.91, close to the 0.92 it costs to satisfy both.

The α dial: clusters by risk, by ESG, or both

Hybrid distance d* = α·dcorr + (1−α)·dESG, eight Ward clusters. At α = 1 clusters reflect co-movement alone; at α = 0, ESG profiles alone. Each row is a cluster; dots are its members' ESG risk, the bar their middle half.

Do cluster caps help a tracking-error optimiser? Not on this universe: the optimised 20% cut already keeps every correlation cluster within 2.5 percentage points of its benchmark weight. Caps repair screens and sorts, which ignore correlations.
